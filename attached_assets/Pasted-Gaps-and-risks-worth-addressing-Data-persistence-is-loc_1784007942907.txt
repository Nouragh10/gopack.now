Gaps and risks worth addressing

Data persistence is localStorage-only. No account/cloud sync means progress vanishes if a browser cache clears or the kid switches devices (very common — school Chromebook vs. home tablet). Given this is aimed at K-8 with a real parent dashboard, parents will expect progress to follow the child.
Family Leaderboard could backfire. Ranking a 2nd grader (Maya) against a 4th grader (Alex) by raw XP isn't an equitable comparison and risks discouraging the younger sibling. Consider normalizing by grade level, problems attempted, or effort rather than raw XP — or making it opt-in.
Streak pressure. Duolingo-style streaks are proven engagement drivers but also a well-documented source of anxiety in kids ("I broke my streak"). A streak-freeze or grace day would soften this without losing the mechanic.
Grown-Up Check math gate is easily beaten by the target user. A 4th or 5th grader who's good at math (the exact kid this app is for) can solve 7×6−12 themselves, defeating the purpose of the gate. Worth either scaling gate difficulty above the oldest profile's grade level or using a non-math gate (e.g., a short reading passage or date-based question).
Accessibility beyond "Large Text." No mention of color-blind-safe palettes (purple/blue/green/pink mascots could collide for some users), screen-reader support, or dyslexia-friendly font option — worth at least a stated intention even if v1 doesn't fully implement it, since this is education software.
Curriculum breadth is unclear. "Addition, Subtraction, Multiply, etc." — for a K-8 claim, is there a plan for fractions, decimals, geometry, and word problems at upper grades, or is this v1 scoped to early arithmetic only? Worth clarifying in the doc itself so scope creep doesn't sneak up on you.
No stated difficulty adaptation logic. Is problem selection adaptive (spaced repetition, gets harder as accuracy improves) or static? This matters a lot for whether "Most Used Approach" and "Accuracy %" stats mean anything pedagogically.
